About

I am a postdoctoral research fellow at the Institute for European Policymaking at Bocconi University in Milan, where I work with Professor Guido Tabellini. My research examines how political power is organized, exercised, and contested in both democratic and non-democratic settings.

Much of my work asks why authoritarian rulers build institutions that appear to constrain them — legislatures, parties, formal channels for opposition — and what those institutions actually do once they exist. A parallel strand looks at political behavior in democracies: how people form attachments to radical parties, and what happens when political discussion is suppressed rather than engaged. I work primarily on post-communist Europe, and I draw on causal inference, formal theory, and computational methods including natural language processing.

I earned my Ph.D. in Political Science from the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill in 2026, with subfields in comparative politics and methodology. I also hold an M.A. in Political Science from UNC and an M.A. in Russian and East European Studies from Indiana University Bloomington. I am always glad to hear from potential collaborators.

Research

Authoritarian Institutions and Power Sharing

Autocrats routinely create legislatures and tolerate organized opposition, which seems to cut against their interest in unconstrained rule. I study when power sharing of this kind actually stabilizes a regime, how it alters the survival prospects of the ruler, and how opposition legislators choose between loyalty and dissent once they are inside the institution.

Political Behavior and the Radical Right

Why do voters support populist radical right parties, and what role does political discussion play in shaping those attachments? Related work examines the downstream effects of restricting conversation about contested political topics, both in classrooms and beyond them — a question with direct implications for how institutions handle disagreement.

European Constitutional Governance

An ongoing book project, with Anna Bassi, on the constitutional foundations of European government: how the formal architecture of the European project structures authority, and what that structure means for accountability and policymaking.
